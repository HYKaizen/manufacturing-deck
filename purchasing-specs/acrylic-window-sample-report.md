## 1. Purpose

This report documents the quality evaluation of a new acrylic window sample submitted for potential use in Escape Trailer production units. The sample was inspected for build quality, hardware durability, functionality, and overall fitness for RV/travel trailer application.

## 3. Defects Identified

### 3.1 Locks & Handles — Not Sturdy

**Severity:** Major  
**Category:** Hardware / Durability

- Lock and handle hardware feels lightweight and flimsy
- Thin plastic construction — not suitable for the repeated open/close cycles expected in RV use
- Fastened with small Phillips screws directly into acrylic — risk of loosening over time with vibration during travel
- Does not inspire customer confidence in build quality

**Evidence:** Photos 3 & 4 — close-up of black plastic cam-lock latch

---

### 3.2 Strut Locks — Only Two Positions

**Severity:** Major  
**Category:** Functionality

- Window struts only lock in two positions: fully open or fully closed
- No intermediate friction hold or multi-position detent
- Users cannot partially open the window for ventilation while limiting exposure to rain or wind
- Competing RV window designs typically offer multi-position or friction-stay struts

**Expected:** Minimum 3–4 locking positions, or continuous friction-stay mechanism

---

### 3.3 Reflective Curtain / Blind — Does Not Slide Smoothly

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Category:** Functionality / Aesthetics

- Reflective curtain/blind creates visible wrinkles when operated
- Material bunches and does not sit flat in the closed position
- Roller mechanism appears undersized relative to the blind material width
- Gap between blind track and frame contributes to poor sliding action
- Will generate customer complaints on appearance and usability

**Evidence:** Photos 1 & 2 — visible wrinkling and bunching of blind fabric within frame

---

### 3.4 Bug Screen — Thin and Flimsy

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Category:** Durability

- Bug screen mesh is noticeably thinner than current production windows
- Feels fragile and prone to tearing with normal use (pushing open, cleaning, accidental contact)
- Does not meet the durability expectations for a travel trailer that will see years of outdoor use

**Expected:** Heavier gauge mesh consistent with RV industry standard

---

### 3.5 Blind Locks — Cheap Plastic, Not Durable

**Severity:** Moderate  
**Category:** Hardware / Durability

- Plastic locking clips for the blinds are lightweight and feel breakable
- Likely to crack or fail within the first year of regular use
- Replacement parts availability is unknown — risk of customer dissatisfaction

**Expected:** Metal or reinforced polymer locking mechanism

---

### 3.6 Window Frame Fitment — Seal & Corner Gaps

**Severity:** Major  
**Category:** Structural / Weatherproofing

- Window frame can be pulled away from mounting surface with minimal force
- Black rubber gasket/seal was found dislodged from its channel
- Visible gap at corner joints — poor mitering or frame separation
- These issues would lead to air leaks, water intrusion, and rattling during travel if installed in production

**Evidence:** Video — frame separation demonstrated by hand, seal visibly out of position

## 5. Disposition

❌ **REJECTED** — Sample does not meet Escape Trailer quality standards.

The window assembly has multiple major and moderate defects across hardware, functionality, and structural categories. In its current form, this product would not withstand the demands of travel trailer use and would generate significant warranty claims and customer dissatisfaction.

---

## 6. Recommended Actions for Supplier

1. **Upgrade lock and handle hardware** to metal or heavy-duty reinforced polymer
2. **Replace strut mechanism** with multi-position friction-stay or detent struts
3. **Redesign blind roller system** — wider roller, proper track fitment to eliminate wrinkling
4. **Increase bug screen mesh gauge** to RV industry standard weight
5. **Replace plastic blind locks** with metal or reinforced clips
6. **Improve frame corner joinery and seal retention** — gasket must remain seated under stress

A revised sample addressing all six items should be submitted for re-evaluation before this product can be considered for production use.
